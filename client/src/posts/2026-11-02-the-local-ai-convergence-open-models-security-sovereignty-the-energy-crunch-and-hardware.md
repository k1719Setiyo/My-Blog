Local AI has been a core part of my thesis since the start of the year, and many of you have heard me make the case. In [Rewiring AI in 2026](https://substratesm.com/post/2026-02-04-rewiring-ai-in-2026 ) (my AI thesis for 2026), I argued that AI was moving beyond static, centralized models and that the "sovereign edge" would turn consumer laptops into capable AI workstations. In May, [Frontier Intelligence, Fixed Cost](https://substratesm.com/post/2026-05-27-frontier-intelligence-fixed-cost ) put numbers on it. Since then, AI has kept moving toward a hybrid world in which open models running on local hardware do far more of the work.

[This new deck](https://localaiconvergence.substratesm.com/ ) that I put together makes that case with updated data from various data providers, funds and my own analysis. It argues that four forces are pulling inference closer to the user: open models, security and sovereignty, the energy crunch, and local hardware.

The core arguments:

* **Open models are close enough, and far cheaper.** On Artificial Analysis's new, tougher v5 index, the best open model trails the leader by 13 points. Yet the best open models cost up to 40x less per token than frontier closed models.
* **Usage is already shifting to open models.**
  * Open model downloads have more than doubled in the past year.
  * Open weights account for ~70% of tokens across OpenRouter's top 50 models.
  * Open model inference now rivals all of Google, at ~108T tokens a day.
* **Local inference is no longer a fringe idea**. At the start of the year, running models locally still sounded like a niche pursuit. Today, versions built to run locally make up a third of all open model downloads.
  * Download of local models has grown 9.2x since July 2025, about 3x faster than regular open model downloads.
* **Closed labs still capture the revenue, and price sensitivity is rising.** The expenditure-weighted price of tokens fell 53% since May, even as GPU rental prices climbed on surging inference demand.
* **Power is the binding constraint**. The U.S. grid is tight, and adding capacity is held up by supply chain and interconnection delays.
* **Workloads are moving to inference, and inference can run locally.** Pre-training has fallen from 67% of frontier-lab compute in early 2024 to 10%, while reinforcement learning and inference now take 90%. Unlike giant training runs, inference does not have to sit in a massive data center. It can run where the user and the data are, which is the core argument for local AI.
* **Adoption is early, and spend is climbing fast.** Only 18% of working-age adults worldwide use AI today. Meanwhile, median AI spend per employee tripled in the past year, and the median OpenAI researcher spends ~$18K a month on coding agents, 1,000x the typical enterprise user.
* **Local is ready.**
  * A laptop can now run a 200 billion parameter model, and a desktop a 1 trillion parameter model.
  * A full local stack of runtimes, routing, security and agents is in place, including Ollama, llama.cpp, LiteLLM and Hermes Agent. The stack is evolving fast and hard.
  * Even self-improving research loops now run on a single Mac with Flint from RSI.

As I wrote in May, local AI doesn't replace the cloud; it changes the enterprise's negotiating position with it. The answer is to use the cloud selectively. Run open models locally for steady, private and high-volume work, and escalate to frontier closed models for the exceptions.

Local inferencing is how we:

* make AI affordable for all without sacrificing intelligence;
* take the energy constraint head-on, both on the tight U.S. grid and across an energy-short Global South;
* keep data and models under our own control;
* turn recurring token spend into hardware that pays for itself.
